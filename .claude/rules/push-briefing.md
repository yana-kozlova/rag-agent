# Push, notifications and the briefing

**"Push" is the schedule, not the transport.** Delivery is Telegram, through the one door in `lib/push/deliver.ts`; Web Push is gone, along with `push_subscriptions`, the service worker, the VAPID keys and `/api/push/{subscribe,vapid-key,send,action}`. A subscription bound notifications to one browser profile, expired without telling anyone, and on iOS existed only for an installed PWA — so briefings were reliably generated and unreliably seen. What makes an account reachable is now `users.telegram_chat_id`, which is also what the dispatchers select on; before, they joined `push_subscriptions` and silently skipped everyone who had never granted browser notifications. The names (`lib/push/`, `/api/push/*`) stay because QStash schedules point at those paths — renaming the routes means re-creating every schedule.

**The briefing is assembled, and nothing in it is generated.** Every line is built by the application from the events, dates and tasks it was given (`scheduleLines`, `dateLines`, `taskLines` in `lib/push/briefing.ts`), and the morning pass makes no LLM call at all. There used to be one model-written sentence above the list, and it was narrowed twice for inventing things: first for padding a one-event day with atmosphere ("the day will be festive, which may get in the way of plans"), then for announcing that "between the daily meeting and the maths class there is only an hour" on a day where those two sat six hours apart — the only one-hour gap that day belonged to a different pair entirely. The second failure is why it was deleted rather than narrowed a third time. The prompt asked for "a clash, a tight gap, a long unbroken stretch" while handing over nothing but a list of start times, so the sentence it commissioned *was* arithmetic over dates — exactly what `weekdayOf`, `dateLines`, `taskLines` and `isSlotWithinHours` were each fixed by moving into the application, except that here there was nowhere to move it to: a gap is only worth mentioning if something judges it worth mentioning, and nothing computes that. The ban on consequence in the same prompt could never hold either, because a prompt that names a tight schedule as the topic leaves the sentence nowhere to end but a consequence. The old 180-character ceiling was a browser-notification constraint and went earlier; `MAX_EVENT_LINES` and `MAX_TITLE` are what remain. The retrospective still generates — it reflects on a week that has happened, which is a different job from asserting something about a day that has not.

**The briefing looks past today, and a repeated title gets no notice.** It printed today's schedule and nothing after it, so the dentist on Thursday was first mentioned on Thursday morning — the one point at which being told is no longer notice. `fetchAheadEvents` reads the calendar from tomorrow to the Sunday of the user's current week (`aheadHorizonDays`) and `aheadLines` prints it under "Далі на тижні", after the schedule and before the dates. It was a rolling seven days at first, which made the heading untrue from Tuesday on: a Friday briefing listed next Wednesday under "this week". The cost is that Sunday's briefing has no block at all — Monday is first mentioned on Monday. The saved dates keep their rolling seven (`BRIEFING_HORIZON_DAYS`), since a birthday on Monday is worth knowing on Sunday whatever the week is called. Printed raw, that block is just the calendar: a daily stand-up is five of the six lines every morning and the appointment worth knowing about falls off the end of `MAX_AHEAD_LINES`. So `aheadEvents` gives a title one line, at its first occurrence, and none at all when it is also on today's schedule — a repeated title is a routine, and a routine needs no announcing. The cost is deliberate and stated: a genuinely separate second meeting under the same name is not announced in advance, and still appears on the morning of its own day. Matching is on the title because `CalendarEvent` carries no `recurringEventId`, and because a routine re-created by hand each week is still a routine. The day label is computed and printed (`завтра`, then `пт, 24.07`), never left to be derived from a timestamp, and an all-day start is read as the calendar date it already is rather than through `Date`, on the `weekdayOf` reasoning. A multi-day event already under way overlaps the window and is dropped (`daysAway < 1`): it began on a day the briefing has already had. The read is its own `try`, after today's: losing the look ahead must not turn a readable today into `events === null`, and when today is unreadable it is not attempted — the block is simply absent under the line that already says why.

**The user's own birthday is said to them, not reported to them.** `dateLines` treated every saved birthday alike, so on the day the account holder was sent "🎂 Яна народилась — сьогодні, виповнюється 34" — a reminder to congratulate somebody, addressed to the somebody. `BriefingDate.self` marks a `birth` whose `subject` is the account holder, by `isSelfName` (`lib/actions/entity-identity.ts`) — the test `resolveSelfName` already applied, asked as a question, so "who is the user" has one answer in the graph and in the briefing; a placeholder word ("користувач", "я") counts even when `users.name` is empty. On the day that row becomes `copy.dates.happyBirthday` at the top of the body and leaves the list; in the days before it stays an ordinary line, since a greeting three days early is a miscount. The greeting goes out above an unreadable calendar too, like the dates it came from. Two limits. A birth with no subject is never greeted — it may well be the user's, but greeting on a guess is worse than a line. And the name match is the graph's, over-eager by design: another person saved under the user's bare first name is read as the user.

**`fetchDayNotes` now has one consumer, and its three cuts still earn their keep.** It was built for the deleted lead sentence and is kept for the proactive scan (`insight-scan.ts`), which matches notes against who the user is meeting; `briefing-run` retrieves once and passes them there only. The cuts stay because the scan is just as capable of asserting something unprompted. Retrieval returns its top *k* because *k* is what it was asked for, not because *k* things were relevant — handing that list on under the heading "notes that may be relevant" turned a day with one all-day birthday into a briefing that reported the user's mood and their coffee from a check-in three days earlier. So: a similarity floor, higher than `getInformation`'s and not admitting lexical-only hits the way that one does — there a marginal hit answers a question the user asked and can be ignored on sight, here it becomes an unprompted assertion about their morning, and the query is a calendar title rather than a question ("День народження" reduces to a prefix matching half a Ukrainian base on a word meaning nothing about today). Wellbeing check-ins are dropped outright, on genre rather than on age, since one from this morning is no better: the tracker records and does not assess, and a paragraph narrating the user's mood back at them at breakfast is assessment, on the most sensitive content in the base. And what survives carries the day it was written, because undated a note from Tuesday reads as this morning — which is exactly how a past mood came back in the present tense.

**An unreadable calendar is not an empty day, and a declined event is not a commitment.** `fetchEventsBetween` returned `[]` both when Google answered "nothing" and when it refused to answer, swallowing every rejection through `Promise.allSettled` without so much as a log — so an account whose refresh token Google had expired got five consecutive mornings of "нічого не заплановано — календар вільний", which is the most confident possible way to be wrong. It now logs each failed calendar and *throws* when every calendar failed, on the same reasoning that makes `listCalendars` throw; a partial failure still returns what was read, because one unreadable shared calendar should cost that calendar and not the morning. `generateBriefing` takes `BriefingEvent[] | null` to carry the distinction the whole way — `[]` is a claim about the day, `null` is the absence of one — and `briefing-run` maps both failures onto `null`, the missing token included, since a refresh token Google has revoked is exactly how this presents. Saved dates still go out under it: they come from the timeline rather than from Google, and a birthday is the one thing a broken calendar must not eat. Separately, an event the user declined stays on their calendar and Google keeps returning it; `isDeclined` had lived in `insights.ts` and guarded only the proactive scan, so the briefing printed a week of meetings its user had said no to. It moved beside `EventAttendee` in `calendar-window.ts` and was applied by `fetchEventsBetween` itself — after the dedupe, since only the copy on the user's own calendar carries `responseStatus` — because a rule that has to be remembered by three callers gets remembered by two. The third caller was `getEvents`, and it did not remember: the whole fix landed on the notification path while the chat kept listing declined meetings as commitments. So `isDeclined` and `EventAttendee` now live in `lib/utils/calendars.ts` next to `isTimeBlock`, dependency-free for the reason written there, and `calendar-window.ts` re-exports them so its existing importers keep their address.

**Notification language is `users.locale` (`uk` | `en`), and only notifications.** All copy — titles, deterministic fallbacks, durations, and the `writeIn` line appended to each generation prompt — lives in `lib/push/copy.ts`; the web UI stays English, because a notification is read where the bot speaks and a settings screen is not. Ukrainian's three plural forms are why counts go through `pluralUk` rather than a ternary. Clock times are `en-GB` in both languages on purpose: digits are digits, and a locale-shifted format only makes the briefing's column of times ragged. An unrecognised stored locale resolves to the default instead of throwing, since it would otherwise fail a cron run that has already paid for an LLM call.

Notification buttons are Telegram inline keyboards. `callback_data` is capped at 64 bytes and there is no session cookie on a press, so an action is a name plus a number (`lib/telegram/callback-data.ts`) and everything else is read back off the message text it was attached to — which is why `renderNotification` joins title and body with a blank line and `splitNotification` cuts on it. `handleCallbackQuery` (`lib/telegram/callbacks.ts`) resolves the user from the chat id exactly as an ordinary message does, then strips the keyboard so a week-old briefing can't be saved twice.

**Cron cadence lives in QStash, not `vercel.json`.** The Hobby plan rejects any cron firing more than once a day, and these endpoints ask "who is due right now?" rather than planning a day ahead — so a daily sweep alone drops nearly every briefing. QStash schedules call the same paths with the same `CRON_SECRET`; the entries left in `vercel.json` are a once-a-day backstop for when QStash is unreachable. Changing a briefing's cadence means editing the QStash schedule, not `vercel.json`.
