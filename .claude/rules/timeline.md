# Timeline

**Prose cannot be sorted by when.** The base could already hold "Артем народився 12 березня 2019" as an embedded, findable note. What it could not do is put it in order: nothing answered "що було у 2022?", nothing knew a birthday was three days out, and a note about a move sat in the same undifferentiated pile as a preference about oat milk. So a date is stored twice, on the `wellbeing_entries` precedent — the note keeps its wording and stays searchable, `timeline_events` keeps the day. `metadata.dates` is the note's own record of what extraction found; the table is the projection of every note's into one series, exactly as `entities` is the projection of every note's `metadata.entities` into one graph. It is written from the same two places, on the same terms: `createResource`/`updateResource` sync it, `replace` drops only that note's rows, and a failure is non-fatal because a note without its dates is still a saved note.

**How much of a date is known, and whether it comes round, are two different columns.** Conflating them is what makes a naive events table wrong within a week. `precision` (`day` | `month` | `year` | `day-month`) says which components of `occurredOn` are real — "ми переїхали у 2022" is a year, and a column that keeps only a `date` turns it into 1 January and then prints it back as if someone had said so. `recurrence` (`none` | `annual`) is orthogonal: a wedding on a known day recurs, a known-to-the-day hospital visit does not. `day-month` is the case that forces the split — "у Андрія день народження 14 березня" gives a real day and month and no year, so its stored year is `PLACEHOLDER_YEAR` (2000, a leap year, because `--02-29` is a real birthday and has to survive being stored), is never printed, and keeps such a date off the historical axis entirely: it has no origin point to place, only occurrences ahead of it. That is also why "виповнюється N" is computed only when the original year is real — an age is a claim about a year nobody recorded. Dates are formatted in `timeZone: 'UTC'`, because a calendar date parsed as an instant is UTC midnight and rendering it west of Greenwich prints the day before.

**The hard part is refusing most dates.** `DATE_EXTRACTION_RULES` (`lib/ai/information-extraction.ts`) is one constant shared by the full extraction and by the dates-only pass the backfill runs, so the two cannot drift. It admits what a person would still name years later and rejects the note's own created-at, times of day, routines, vague futures, and ordinary appointments — those live in the calendar, which already holds them. A knowledge base whose timeline fills with "the meeting is on Tuesday" has a timeline nobody opens twice. The prompt is given the user's own today (`todayFor`), not the server's: at 01:00 in Kyiv the server is still on yesterday, and a relative date filed a day early is never noticed and never corrected. `MAX_DATES_PER_NOTE` caps what one import can contribute — a document about a war produces forty dates, none of them the user's.

`rememberDate` exists beside all that because extraction cannot produce a yearless date from prose reliably: asked for "день народження 14 березня" it guesses a year, and the birthday quietly becomes a one-off in 2026. Stating it outright used to be the only way to *correct* a date, since a second note about the same day is a second note.

**A date read out of prose is wrong in ordinary ways, and correcting it had to cost the evidence.** Most rows here were written by a model reading a note, so they are wrong the way models are wrong — the right day off by one, the subject's name left in the title, a birthday filed under the day the note was written — and the only repair on offer was delete-and-retype, which produces a `manual` row with no `resource_id` and therefore no link back to the note that is the evidence for it. So the honest correction cost the provenance and keeping the provenance meant living with the wrong day. `updateTimelineEvent` is the third verb, a route rather than a tool on the same reasoning that keeps deletion out of the model's hands: quietly rewriting the day something happened is a change nobody reviews.

Two things make a correction stick and neither is enough alone. `timeline_events.edited_at` exempts the row from the wholesale replace in `syncTimelineForResource` — an `extraction` row is a projection of `metadata.dates`, rebuilt every time anything folds a fact into that note, so without the flag a fixed date reverts days later with nothing raising. That is `entities.relationship_source` one step smaller: there the sync updates a surviving row and the flag buys exemption from an overwrite, here it deletes and rebuilds and the flag buys exemption from the delete. And the note's own `metadata.dates` is restated to match, because that list is what the sync rebuilds *from*: leave it saying 31 August and the next re-save inserts the model's original as a second row beside the corrected one, the identity index having no reason to call them the same event. `rewriteDateOnResource` is one function for that and for the deletion case, since dropping an entry and replacing one are the same operation against the same list and the interesting half is the matching. It writes nothing when no entry matches, `next` or not — the list has already drifted at that point, and appending a date the note's prose may not support to fix a projection is editing the evidence to match the conclusion.

`edited_at` is a timestamp rather than a boolean because it is also what the chip on the axis reads, and `source` cannot say this: `source` records where the date *came from*, which a correction does not change. The chip therefore sits beside the source link rather than beside the title — the date still came from that note, it is just no longer the note's reading of it. The form is one component (`app/timeline/DateForm.tsx`) shared by adding a date and correcting one, and `toDateSpec`/`splitDateSpec` moved into `lib/timeline/timeline.ts` to be tested there: the awkward part is not the markup but which combinations of day, month and year mean anything, and a second copy of that is a second answer to a question `precision` exists to have exactly one answer to. `splitDateSpec` reads `precision` and not the stored date, so a year-only row opens with its month and day blank — handing back the padding 1 January is how opening the form and saving it untouched promotes a year into a day somebody named. `resolveRecurrence` is the same consolidation for the recurrence rule, which had two spellings and was about to get a third. Deleting is an API route, not a tool, on the wellbeing precedent. Identity is `(user_id, occurred_on, kind, subject_key, lower(btrim(title)))` — deliberately conservative: it will not catch "Артем народився" against "народження Артема", and that is the safer direction to fail, because a visible duplicate can be deleted and a silently swallowed second event on the same day cannot be recovered. `resource_id` cascades (the note is the evidence) while `entity_id` sets null (losing a node is no reason to forget when their child was born), and a date the user typed has neither, so nothing that happens in the knowledge base can touch it.

Upcoming dates ride into the morning briefing through `dateLines`, built by the application exactly as `scheduleLines` is — a model told a birthday is in three days will eventually say two. An empty calendar no longer means an empty briefing: a birthday tomorrow is the whole reason to send anything on a day with nothing scheduled. The horizon there is a week rather than the page's two months, because an anniversary announced every morning for six weeks stops being read.
